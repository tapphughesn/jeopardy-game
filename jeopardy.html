Rules & judging guide

Quick host rulings for the situations most likely to cause confusion.

1. Form of a question

  • Responses should be phrased as a question: “What is…?” or “Who is…?” Grammar does not need to be perfect.
  • In the first Jeopardy round, accept one lapse and remind the player. In Double Jeopardy, Daily Doubles, and Final Jeopardy, missing the question form is incorrect.

2. Buzzing, silence & rebounds

  • Players should wait until the clue is fully read. The host decides who buzzed first.
  • No buzz means no penalty. Once recognized, an incorrect response or failure to answer loses the clue value.
  • After a wrong response, the other players may buzz in. Do not reveal the answer until everyone has declined or time expires.

3. What counts as correct?

  • The response must satisfy both the clue and its category. Category instructions such as “starts with B” are part of the clue.
  • A surname is usually enough when it identifies only one reasonable person. Say “Be more specific” when clarification is genuinely needed.
  • A player may correct themself until the host rules or time runs out. Extra information that contradicts the correct response makes it wrong.

4. Pronunciation & spelling

  • Accept a spoken response that is recognizably the same answer, including a reasonable phonetic pronunciation.
  • Do not accept a change that adds or removes meaningful sounds, changes a title, or produces a different name or word.
  • Final Jeopardy spelling can be imperfect if it is phonetically correct, unless spelling is specifically tested.

5. Daily Doubles

  • Only the player who selected the clue may respond; there are no rebounds.
  • The wager is locked before the clue appears. This game allows $5 up to the greater of the player’s score or the highest clue value in that round.
  • A correct response adds the wager; an incorrect response or no response subtracts it.

6. Scoring & board control

  • Correct responses add the clue value; incorrect recognized responses subtract it.
  • The last player to respond correctly chooses the next clue. After a triple stumper, the player who chose the previous clue keeps control.
  • If a ruling changes, use “Undo score” or “Adjust scores” before continuing.
Final Jeopardy in this game: Only players with a positive score participate. Wagers range from $0 up to the greater of the player’s score or $1,000. Give players about 30 seconds; responses must be in question form. The host’s judgment is final, but consistency matters more than strictness in a home game.
